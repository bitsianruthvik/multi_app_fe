Work in progress by stage and age

Recorded per batch. Age = days since the pieces completed the previous step (oldest first). Double-click any figure for the batch list (with WO no., order and heats; double-click a line there to trace it). Stale = pieces left on a batch that has had no ERP entry of any kind for over 180 days; these are often pieces scrapped or lost without an entry and should be checked physically.

Inside "HT Completed – Inspection (QC) Pending": estimated split (indicative only)

The ERP does not record fettling or machining against the batch, so this split is worked out by part: pieces completed at one step minus pieces issued to the next, oldest first, counting movements from 1 Apr 2025. Rows marked Estimate carry an error margin: the share of pieces that did not balance when the same arithmetic was run on parts that had finished moving through (back-test on ERP history). Rows marked Definite come straight from challans. Customer filter applies; order type and metal type filters do not (challans carry only the part).

Out with fettling contractors and vendors

Issued and not yet received back, from challans. Challans record the part, not the batch, so these are shown separately and not added to the stage totals. Age = days since issued. Suspect = open line where a later challan for the same part, vendor and process has already come back in full; often a receipt booked against the wrong challan or a challan never closed, so confirm with the vendor before chasing. Order type and metal type filters do not apply here.

Vendors and contractors: material out, and for how long

Vendor / contractor
Challans record the part, not the batch. Age = days since sent. Suspect = open line where a later challan for the same part, vendor and process is already fully back (probably a receipt booked against the wrong challan); confirm with the vendor before chasing. "Days of work queued" = open quantity ÷ average sent per day in the last 90 days; high values point to an overloaded vendor. Order type and metal type filters do not apply to challans (they carry only the part).

Machining and turning scrap

Vendor

Scrap = the scrap weight entered when material comes back from the vendor (metal removed). It is entered on only some receipt lines (almost none from Apr 2025 to Feb 2026), so scrap % is calculated only on lines where scrap was entered, and the coverage % shows how complete the entry is. Scrap % = scrap ÷ casting weight received (pieces × the die casting weight). Scrap is metal removed only: rejected pieces are not in it (shown separately below). Customer filter applies (by part); order type and metal type do not.

Quality analysis

Process (where caught)
Rejection reason

In-house rejections recorded per batch. Each reason in the ERP belongs to a responsible department (for example "Shell breakage_Coating" = Coating), even when it is caught later at melting or inspection. Pick processes first; the reason list then shows only reasons recorded at those processes. Customer, order type, metal type and kg/pieces filters apply.

Rejection % over time

Pareto: which reasons matter most

Bars = rejected; line = cumulative share of all rejections in the selection. Reasons to the left of 80% are where to focus.

Responsible department vs target

Rejection % = rejected (by responsible department) ÷ pieces poured in the period. Targets are the ERP's department rejection-control values.

Where defects are caught vs where they are caused

Rows = process where the rejection was recorded; columns = department responsible. Figures far from the "same stage" column mean defects travelled downstream (adding cost) before being caught.

Trend for one stage

Vendor performance: speed and quality

Speed: kg received back per working day, and per working day while the vendor was holding our material (shows the vendor's real rate when loaded). Compare with the pour rate: a vendor that a whole process depends on must keep up with it. Quality: receipts never record rejections except the in-house fettling contractors, so a rejection at final or machining inspection is charged to the vendor who returned that part last before it (the latest vendor event). Tick "Machining reasons only" to leave casting defects out.

Each bubble is a vendor and process: right = slower, up = more rejections, size = kg received. Click a bubble for its charged rejections.

Capacity: in-house + outsourced, per step

Capacity used = average kg processed per working day at each step: in-house entries plus what came back from vendors. Heat treatment splits its final pass into in-house and at HT vendors. A step running below what is poured each day builds a queue in front of it. Double-click a step for its weekly figures.
